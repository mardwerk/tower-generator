| Review verdict | The review's judgment of one subject that code requires a judgment of, given apart from its at most eight other findings: each whole-technique omission in the plan's `omittedTechniques`, whether a supported typed change or a Proposed mechanic on a purchase could adapt its central effect, a literal detail the Definition cannot express justifying the omission of that aspect only, what the Unit already covers of that effect and what is absent, whether the plan still names the technique elsewhere, and whether its rank fits the passages it cites, each Required concept of the Request, whether a typed change carries its central effect, each path's third purchase, its Path identity, each path's fifth purchase, judged with the other two fifth purchases, its path's identity and its payoff against its price, and each Proposed mechanic of a purchase, `unresolved` when it is a coherent, source-fitting capability a player could see and `fail` otherwise, never `pass`. The view (`authoringIssues`) and `render --details` list the authoring checks that fail today as current checks, apart from the stored Findings. |  | Pending local review | Tower Generator | To be reviewed |

Findings, usage and provenance are rendered apart from it (`render --details`). |  | Pending local review | Tower Generator | To be reviewed |

| Source techniques | The techniques and forms a prepared request's selected source passages name, found by fixed rules (power section headings, leading terms of entries, technique page titles, signature sentences), each with its passage IDs, its aliases when one passage proves two names are one technique, and its salience (`sourceTechniques`). `prepare` derives them; they are evidence for the plan, not a Repertoire. | Salience | How much source evidence a source technique has, `strong` or `normal` (`salience`), derived by fixed rules: strong when research followed a technique or form page titled for it, when a signature cue names it, or when its own sections and entries hold at least a tenth of the character article's power and ability sections. It is evidence that a technique is at least major, never by itself that it is a Core concept: under `requireCoreConcepts` a plan that ranks a strong technique minor fails. |  | Pending local review | Tower Generator | To be reviewed |
| Repertoire | The character's source-backed techniques and forms that a design plan selects (`repertoire`). Each planned purchase names the repertoire technique or base attack it adapts, and every entry is used: it is named like the base attack or is the technique of at least one purchase, and a technique that neither adapts belongs in `omittedTechniques` with its reason, where the review gives it a verdict (`RepertoireUseRule`). Every path has a technique of its own; two paths share one only as crosspath synergy. Under `requireCoreConcepts` each entry, and each technique the plan omits, carries its `importance`: `core`, `major` or `minor`. |  | Pending local review | Tower Generator | To be reviewed |
| Core concept | A Repertoire entry ranked `core`: one of the one to three concepts without which the character would not feel canonical, judged from the sources. Under the design policy's `requireCoreConcepts` it is the base attack or the technique of at least one purchase that adapts its central effect with a typed change, and it is never omitted whole, only in named aspects. One adapted only by a Proposed mechanic is a Design gap: no build grants a proposal, so the Unit embodies the concept only once the Definition supports that mechanic. A purchase that carries its name but not what it does in play does not adapt it; the review judges that. Its central effect, as a Required concept's, is the source-backed effect that identifies it against the character's other forms and techniques: a stat it shares with them cannot alone carry it. |  | Pending local review | Tower Generator | To be reviewed |
| Required concept | A technique or form of the character that its Request requires the Unit to adapt (`requiredConcepts: [{name, reason?}]`), the owner's explicit decision, not a Profile rule: the plan names it as the base attack or as a Repertoire entry that is the technique of at least one purchase adapting its central effect, as a typed change or a Proposed mechanic of that purchase, and never omits it whole. Code checks the plan, and on the checked Unit reports one of any rank that only Proposed mechanics adapt as a Design gap; the review gives each a Review verdict: `pass` only when a typed change carries its central effect, naming that purchase and change, `fail` when the Unit adapts it only in name, only for a peripheral effect or only with stats its cited passages do not make its identifying effect, `unresolved` when only a coherent, source-fitting Proposed mechanic carries it. |  | Pending local review | Tower Generator | To be reviewed |
| Early benefits | What a path's first and second purchases improve or unlock, taken together: the multiset of their improvement dimensions and unlocks. Order is ignored, a dimension both purchases improve counts twice, and `lowers`, names, prices and amounts are left out. Under the design policy's `distinctEarlyBenefits`, no two paths may have the same early benefits. Under `exclusiveEarlyBenefits`, which subsumes it, no two paths' early benefits may share a dimension or unlock: an unlock of a dimension a purchase can also improve, such as splash, counts as that dimension, and a targeting change is left out. |  | Pending local review | Tower Generator | To be reviewed |
| Path identity | What a path's third purchase adds that makes the path its own. It can be simple, such as the only purchase that adds projectiles, or a new form, and a dimension another path's purchase also raises in passing does not take it away. The review judges it from the rules document's purchase roles, comparing the third purchase with its own path's first and second purchases and with the other paths' purchases, and records a Review verdict on each third purchase; code checks no Path identity, and `exclusiveEarlyBenefits` is the typed floor under it. |  | Pending local review | Tower Generator | To be reviewed |
| Proposed mechanic | A Mechanic proposal that one purchase needs, kept on that purchase (`proposedMechanics: [{name, effect, sourceIds}]` on its tier) beside the typed changes that approximate it: `effect` says what it does in play and `sourceIds` cite the passages that describe it. No build grants it and build resolution ignores it. Each is reported as an unresolved `proposed-mechanic` Finding, flagged for Definition expansion, and the review fails one that does not fit its purchase and sources, is not a playable mechanic or is not player-visibly distinct from a supported mechanic its purchase already has (`ProposalDistinctRule`). A plan milestone may name one, and its purchase keeps it. It can be a third purchase's Path identity, but it does not meet `requireTier3BehaviorChange` or `requireTier5BehaviorChange`: a purchase whose only new capability is proposed is a Design gap. |  | Pending local review | Tower Generator | To be reviewed |
| Design gap | Something the design policy or the Request requires that a Unit has only as a Proposed mechanic: a third or fifth purchase that a behavior rule (`requireTier3BehaviorChange`, `requireTier5BehaviorChange`) covers, that adds no supported behavior or access and whose only new capability is a Proposed mechanic; under `requireCoreConcepts`, a Core concept that the purchases whose technique it is adapt only with Proposed mechanics; or a Required concept, of any rank, that the purchases whose technique it is adapt only with Proposed mechanics. It is not playable until the Definition supports the mechanic. It does not fail generation: it is reported as an unresolved Finding, `proposed-capability` for a purchase, `core-concept` for a Core concept and `required-concept` for a Required concept that is not also a Core concept with that Finding. A purchase, Core concept or Required concept with neither a supported adaptation nor a Proposed mechanic fails its rule. |  | Pending local review | Tower Generator | To be reviewed |
| Unsupported mechanic | Behavior a unit's sources call for that its Definition cannot express. When a purchase needs it, it is a Proposed mechanic of that purchase; otherwise it is recorded as a Mechanic proposal in the blueprint's `proposals` and reported as an `unsupported-mechanic` Finding. No build grants it. |  | Pending local review | Tower Generator | To be reviewed |
| Bonus damage | A typed, additive +N that an attack adds to each hit on enemies with one enemy property, such as +50 against Hardened. Purchases only add to it: two paths' additions sum, and damage multipliers do not scale it. A Definition's vocabulary lists the properties that accept it (`bonusDamageProperties`); the Default Profile lists Hardened, its adaptation of BTD6 Ceramic, and Blimp, its adaptation of BTD6 MOAB-class, as separate properties: a Blimp is not Hardened, and each property's bonus is its own. A damage type that cannot hurt a property blocks its bonus damage too. BTD6's class bonuses, such as Deadly Precision's +50 to Ceramic and MOAB Press's +4 to MOAB-class, are what it adapts. |  | Pending local review | Tower Generator | To be reviewed |
